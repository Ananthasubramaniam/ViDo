The working vision

Simple enough for anyone. Structured enough for serious work.

ViDo begins with the familiar act of creating and completing a task. Its ambition is to organize that simplicity so thoughtfully that the same product can support a homemaker, an individual, a student, a professional, or eventually a business team.

Universal Professional Minimal Deadline-aware Private first Collaborative later
North-star principle
Power should come from organization—not from putting more controls on every screen.

Inspired by how products such as Zoho Projects turn a basic to-do concept into something trusted for serious work without making information feel chaotic.

Design principles

What ViDo should protect

These are working principles, not final branding statements.

01

Universal language

Avoid terminology that assumes office work. The same interaction should make sense for groceries, schoolwork, maintenance, or client delivery.

02

Clarity before density

Organize information through sections, hierarchy, and progressive disclosure instead of displaying every property at once.

03

Motivation without guilt

Help users act on overdue work without confronting them with a discouraging wall of red tasks or punitive language.

04

Professional and minimal

Use restrained visuals, predictable interactions, strong spacing, and clear typography rather than decoration or gamification.

05

Simple states

Tasks begin with only Active and Completed. Today, Upcoming, and Needs Attention are calculated views, not extra statuses to maintain.

06

Build capability gradually

Private tasks establish the core. Shared tasks, richer organization, reminders, dark mode, and AI arrive only when their foundations are clear.

Still unresolved

Questions we should see before answering

These decisions should be tested through small visual alternatives rather than forced verbally.

Motivational tone Open

Calm and reassuring, energetic and celebratory, or highly practical? The current preference is to avoid guilt, but the exact tone needs visual testing.

Task-card density Open

Minimal cards versus balanced cards. The Home dashboard may need less detail than dedicated task views.

Recurring-task behavior Future

Deferred from Phase 1. When revisited, daily routines must stay visible without generating clutter or pushing ordinary tasks out of place.

Ordering calculation Open

Tasks should be ordered usefully through a clear calculation, but the balance between deadlines and priority is undecided.

Brand accent Open

Blue, indigo, green, or nearly monochrome should be compared through restrained interface samples.

Landing-page story Open

One universal message supported by examples is the current recommendation; exact sections and copy remain to be discussed.

Evolution

Build slowly, preserve the foundation

Phase boundaries will be refined after the first experience is fully planned.

Foundation — Private tasks

Landing page, Google sign-in, private Supabase storage, Home dashboard, task creation, deadlines, optional priority, completion, archive, and deletion.

Organization — Lists, tags, and routines

Custom lists, tags, refined recurring tasks, ordering controls, and denser dedicated views.

Collaboration — Shared tasks

Sharing, ownership, invitations, comments, permissions, and eventually team workspaces.

Intelligence — Embedded AI agent

Context-aware task suggestions, scheduling assistance, risk detection, and recommendations grounded in user-approved data.
